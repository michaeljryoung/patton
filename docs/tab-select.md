# Patton — Tab strip: working mark and multi-select (S63)

Two tab-strip features Michael picked on the try-out page `docs/mocks/tryout-s63-tabs.html` (24 Nov 2026), drawn there from Patton's own tab styles. Its CSS and its section-3 behaviour are the agreed look and behaviour; this file records where the page was silent and what was chosen.

## D1 — W1: a still grey dot on a tab where Claude is mid-turn

**His pick:** "W1 · Still dot" ("A small grey dot that never moves: the quietest of the three"), over W2 (a turning arc) and W3 (three dots brightening in turn).

**What it does.** A tab with a pane where Claude is mid-turn shows a 5 px dot in the strip's muted ink (`--muted`) in the mark slot, with 2 px either side so the 9 px slot never shifts. It is the lowest mark: a flag (manual, or the automatic `WAKE` yellow), the red checkpoint circle, the green needs-you dot and the parked ring all take the slot first. It shows on the active tab too, as on the page (betterterm, the tab you're on, carries it there).

**Where it comes from.** `Pane.midTurn` = Claude running in the pane (a non-Claude program's 7501 `working` shows nothing) and working, not asking (a permission or question reads as `blocked`, the green dot's business) and not past a turn that ended over a still-working root (the ring's). That last stop holds only with the transcript feed attached: `handleHookStop` sets `turnEnded` only when the feed is attached and no other Stop hook blocked the stop, the same limit as the ring itself; without a feed, a Stop over a still-working root leaves the dot showing until the root reports done. `Tab.working` is true when any pane of the tab is mid-turn; `TabManager.updateTabBar` sends it as `working`; `tab-bar.ts` draws `.tab-bar-tab-indicator.working` (aria-label "Claude working"; the tab's own label gains ", Claude working"). Not the dot's activity word (`currentActivity`), which is ranked for alerting (CLAUDE.md Gotchas). The tab asks the strip to redraw only when its answer changes (`Tab.noteWorking`, from the pane's checkpoint key, which holds working, blocked and the turn end), and one `[TAB] working {tabId, on}` line is logged per change of the tab's answer (never per redraw). When the change coincides with a title change (Claude starting or leaving), `refreshTitle` records it, so the strip redraws once.

**Where the page was silent:**
- A folded group's chip and the hover list under it show the same dot: `working` is the lowest rank of `stateOf`/`strongestState` (below the parked ring), so a hidden tab that is mid-turn marks the chip only when nothing higher applies; the chip's label adds "N Claude working".
- No motion and no reduced-motion variant are needed: the dot never moves.

Check: `scripts/check-tab-working-mark.mjs` (the real TabBar: every priority pair, the active tab, labels, the folded chip and its hover list, every rank against it, no log per redraw; split panes, closing the working pane, Claude leaving mid-turn, a Stop over a working root with and without the feed, a non-Claude program's `working`, one redraw when the dot and the title change together; the real TabManager, Tab and Pane fed 7501 records and window titles through a fake PTY: working, blocked, done, a turn ending over background work, the title path's ◐ and ✳, no redraw from a repeated report).

## D2 — Multi-select: S1, a light tint with a thin outline

**His words:** "ability to select multiple tabs at once for putting in groups or moving at once - shift and click and selects from first to last ... cmd click to select individually then be able to move or group as whole, or colour as whole, rename shouldnt apply to whole bunch". **His pick:** S1 ("Tint and outline"), over S2 (a top line) and S3 (a check mark).

**What it does** (the page's section 3, built in the real strip):
- A plain click opens a tab, and it alone is selected. ⌘-click adds or takes out a tab without opening it; ⇧-click selects every tab on the strip from the anchor to the clicked one, without opening any. The anchor is the last plain- or ⌘-clicked tab, as on the page. The tab you're on always counts and keeps its look; only the others draw `.multi`.
- Esc, or a click on the empty strip or the drag space beside +, goes back to the tab you're on alone. Right-click a selected tab for what acts on them all: "Add N Tabs to New Group" (the name box opens on the new chip), "Add N Tabs to <group>" (N = those not in it yet; a group holding them all is left out), "Remove N Tabs from Group", "Flag N Tabs: Green / Yellow / Red / None", "Remove Tab from Selection" (not on the tab you're on, which always counts), and "Rename Tab…" marked "this tab only" (on every tab, a tab named for its project included, as on the single-tab menu: 25 Nov). Right-click an unselected tab: the single-tab menu as before, ending with "Add Tab to Selection" (not on the tab you're on).
- Dragging a selected tab's grip carries the whole selection in strip order, under a "N tabs" picture. Dropped on a tab: into the slot under the pointer (the left half of the tab is before it, the right half after it), all taking that slot's group, by the rule in `docs/tab-groups.md` (24 Nov 2026: a group's right edge joins it; the first rule, "the target's slot and group", is gone). Dropped on a chip's right half: added to that group; on its left half (25 Nov): out of every group, as one block just before it. Dropped on the empty strip, the + button or the drag space (the page's `end` target): to the end of the strip, in their order, out of any group, with a 2 px line at the strip's right edge while over it; this holds for one tab dragged alone too. A group the move empties goes, as with any move.

**Where it lives.** `TabBar` keeps the selection (`selectedExtra`, `selectAnchor`, `clickTab`, `pruneSelection`, `selectionMenuItems`, `onEscapeKey`); `TabManager` acts (`addTabsToNewGroup`, `addTabsToGroup`, `removeTabsFromGroups`, `setTabsFlag`, `dropTabs`) through the block helpers in `services/tab-groups.ts` (`insertBlock`, `gatherAsNewGroup`, `gatherAfterGroup`, `takeBlockOutOfGroup`, and `dropPlacement` for a drop), so every group stays one unbroken run. The look is `.tab-bar-tab.multi` in `tabs.css` on the tokens `--tab-sel` (accent in light, focus colour in dark), `--tab-sel-mix` and `--tab-sel-mix-hover` (12 / 20 % light, 20 / 28 % dark), as on the page. Logs: `[TAB] selection {count}` only when the selection changes (count includes the tab you're on), `[TAB] moved together {count, to}`, `[TAB] flagged together {count, flag}`, `[GROUP] created {tabs, fromGroups}`, `[GROUP] tabs added|removed {tabs}`, `[GROUP] tabs moved between groups {count, to, by: 'drag'}`.

**Where the page was silent:**
- Esc clears only when the key comes from the strip or the page itself. An Esc typed in a terminal goes to Claude (it interrupts a turn) and leaves the selection; so does an Esc that cancels a rename box.
- ⌘ only, not Ctrl: on the Mac a Ctrl-click is a right-click. Chromium sends its press as a left mousedown before the contextmenu, so a Ctrl press (without ⌘) on a tab, a chip, the empty strip or the drag space is ignored: it opens the menu and does nothing else (no tab opened, no selection cleared, no fold, no window drag). ⌘-Ctrl stays a ⌘-click.
- Menu items build a selection without ⌘ or ⇧ (Michael prefers what he can see to shortcuts): "Add Tab to Selection" and "Remove Tab from Selection" on a tab's menu act as a ⌘-click (the added tab becomes the ⇧ anchor); "Select Group's Tabs" on a chip's menu adds an open group's tabs to the selection (added, not replacing, so two groups can be gathered; shown disabled on a folded group, whose tabs are off the strip). From the keyboard, Shift+F10 or the menu key on a chip reaches it; on tabs, the arrow keys open the tab they move to, which drops the selection, so a tab's own items are for the mouse.
- At the 50-group limit, "Add N Tabs to New Group" (and "Add Tab to New Group") still works when the tabs hold the whole of a group, since that group empties and the count doesn't grow.
- A strip rebuild during a drag (a title tick) keeps the carried tabs drawn dragging, and the drop still acts on them; a carried tab closed meanwhile is skipped. A drag whose dragend never arrived ends at the next press on the strip.
- Any change of the tab you're on by another route (⌘1–9, ⌘⇧], the palette, a closed tab, a bookmark) drops the selection, as does a plain click. Closed tabs and tabs hidden by folding their group leave it at the next redraw, and unfolding doesn't bring them back. ⇧ counts only tabs on the strip, so it never selects into a folded group.
- A window drag from a tab, the empty strip or the drag space changes no selection: only a press that doesn't move the window counts as a click.
- Flag N Tabs sets the flag on every one; picking the ticked flag again keeps it (a single tab's menu toggles, but a mixed set would toggle unevenly). "Flag N Tabs: None" clears all.
- Move Left / Move Right, bookmarking and "Set Default Tab Name for Project…" act on one tab, so the selection's menu leaves them out; right-click an unselected tab, or select just one, for them.
- A drag from another window is ignored here as before (tab ids repeat across windows, CLAUDE.md Gotchas): a multi-selection never crosses windows.
- A group made from the selection sits where its first tab was; if that was inside another group's run, just past that run's end. Taking tabs out of a group puts them just after its remaining tabs (where they are when none remain).
