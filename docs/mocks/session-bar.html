Patton Session Bar

Today, what Claude has running in the background is hidden at the bottom of its screen. The session bar sits on the right of every Claude tab and lists it, with how long each job has run, what failed, the links Claude has given you, and the checkpoint. A button folds it away when you want the width back.

Version 3, with your choices from 22 Nov: permanent, with a minimize button rather than a shortcut; no checklist and no changes; failures and links added; the checkpoint moved here from the top bar. Modelled on the Claude desktop app's session details panel. The sketch's rows come from this morning's work; the monitor, loop and failure rows are made up to show those kinds.

What the logs and probes show

  • Work is common and long. From 19 to 22 Nov in main.log, some tab had an agent or workflow running 45% of the time: 434 busy stretches (about 840 agents, since overlapping ones merge), a median of 4 minutes each. Background work of any kind, agents included, left some tab on the hollow ring 79% of the time.
  • Why shells hide today. Claude Code's status reports carry agents, workflows, teammates and cloud sessions, but never shells, monitors or loops: its code lists the four kinds it reports. That is likely also why a tab can show done while an agent's own shell still runs.
  • A bug found along the way. The Stop hook meant to ignore Claude's auto-mode scan and memory import, but matched internal names while Claude sends display names, so either could turn a finished tab's dot into the hollow ring. Fixed and released as v1.34.1.
  • Not possible. Patton can't stop a job, as the desktop app's pane can, because the job belongs to Claude Code. Stopping stays with Claude.

Decided

  • Permanent on Claude tabs, with a minimize button, not a shortcut.
  • Running, Scheduled, Failures, Links and Checkpoint; no checklist, no changes.
  • Shells and monitors live, through a second hook.
  • The checkpoint clock and its button move here from the top bar.
  • Links keep the newest: the same link moves up; a newer version of a file replaces the old one.
